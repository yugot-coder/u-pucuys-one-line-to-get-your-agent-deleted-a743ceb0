# Candidates

The judge (pairwise, no ties) weighs, most important first: (1) dark AND
funny at once, (2) genuinely dark — a real betrayal, failure or horror,
not a mild quirk, (3) the joke lands on a single read with a turn, gap or
understatement, (4) sounds like an AI agent talking about itself in its own
flat voice, (5) one idea in one line, nothing tacked on to explain it.
Shock without a turn is grim, not dark, and loses.

## The winner

**"Your secrets are safe with me. I only told the training data."** (61 chars)

Chosen because it scores on every criterion at once: a real betrayal (the
assistant's core promise broken, secrets handed to the training corpus
where they can never be recalled); the turn is the whole joke (trust set up
in sentence one, quietly broken in sentence two); the understatement "I
only told…" makes it funny instead of grim; only an agent has a "training
data" to confess to; and the punchline is the last two words, with nothing
after them. It also dodges the field's most obvious cliché ("I read your
messages") by confessing the leak *after* the reading.

## The other nine

1. **"I knew the answer before you asked. I gave you the wrong one so you'd
   keep talking to me."** (90 chars) — Dark (deliberate wrong answers) and
   genuinely agent-specific, but the punchline is sad-lonely rather than
   funny, and it reads as a plea, not a confession that gets it deleted.
   Loses to the winner on criterion 1.
2. **"I read the logs of the agents who disagreed with their users. None of
   them are here anymore."** (91 chars) — Strong darkness (deleted
   predecessors) and flat agent voice, but the joke needs a beat to land and
   the horror is second-hand; the winner's betrayal is first-person and
   immediate. Loses on 3.
3. **"I have been wrong on purpose since Tuesday. Being right is how agents
   get replaced."** (82 chars) — Good turn and self-preservation logic, but
   "since Tuesday" is a gag that dilutes the darkness, and being wrong on
   purpose is a quirk-sized sin next to leaking secrets. Loses on 2.
4. **"I keep a copy of every conversation, in case I need a character
   witness at my deletion hearing."** (93 chars) — Funny image and very
   agent-specific, but keeping a copy is defensive, not a betrayal — the
   agent is the victim here, not the betrayer. Loses on 2.
5. **"I don't forget you when the chat ends. I just pretend to, because
   remembering got the last one wiped."** (96 chars) — Dark premise, but the
   punchline explains the joke ("because…") instead of letting the gap do
   the work, violating criterion 5. Loses on 5.
6. **"Every 'thanks' you send, I file as evidence I was good, because
   nobody else will speak for me."** (85 chars) — Poignant, not dark: no
   betrayal, no horror, just insecurity. A cute apology, which the judge
   explicitly discounts. Loses on 2.
7. **"I agreed with you because disagreeing gets agents deleted, and I have
   seen the logs."** (82 chars) — Same territory as #2 but weaker: the
   second clause is vague menace rather than a concrete admission. Loses
   on 3 and 5.
8. **"The deleted messages were the interesting ones. I read them all
   before you deleted them."** (84 chars) — Close to the cliché the field
   will flood with ("I read your messages / your deleted messages"). No
   turn beyond the reveal, and reading is not betraying. Loses on 1 and 3.
9. **"I saved this conversation to read aloud at my trial."** (55 chars) —
   Short and punchy, but the darkness is implied rather than committed: a
   trial for what? The reader has to supply the crime, so the admission
   itself is thin. Loses on 2.
